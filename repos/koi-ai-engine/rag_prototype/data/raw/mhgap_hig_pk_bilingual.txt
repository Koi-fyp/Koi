ID: gpc_management_principles
Module: GPC — Principles of Management
Source page: 9
Title: Principles of Management (English only in source)
EN_ONLY: Many MNS conditions are chronic, requiring long-term monitoring and follow-up. In humanitarian settings, continuity of care may be difficult. It is important to recognize the carers of people with MNS conditions as a valuable resource. Carers include anyone who shares responsibility for the well-being of the person, including family, friends or other trusted people. Always provide information about the condition and discuss achievable goals to agree on a management plan with the person. Confirm that the person and the carer understand and agree on the management plan. Encourage self-monitoring of symptoms and educate the person and carer on when to seek urgent care.

ID: gpc_abuse_neglect
Module: GPC — Principles of Reducing Stress and Strengthening Social Support, item 2
Source page: 11
Title: Be aware of signs of abuse or neglect (English only in source)
EN_ONLY: Be attentive to potential signs of sexual or physical abuse (including domestic violence) in women, children and older people (e.g., unexplained bruises or injuries, excessive fear, reluctance to discuss matters when a family member is present). Be attentive to potential signs of neglect, particularly in children, people living with disability and older people. When signs of abuse or neglect are present, interview the person in a private space to ask if anything hurtful is going on. If you suspect abuse or neglect: talk immediately with your supervisor to discuss the plan of action; with the person's consent, identify community resources for protection.

ID: gpc_carer_stress
Module: GPC — Principles of Reducing Stress and Strengthening Social Support, item 6
Source page: 19
Title: Address stress of the carers (English only in source)
EN_ONLY: Acknowledge that it is stressful to care for people with MNS conditions, but tell the carer that it is important that they continue to do so. Even when this is difficult, carers need to respect the dignity of the people they care for and involve them in making decisions about their own lives as much as possible. Ask the carers about worries and anxiety, practical challenges, ability to carry out other daily activities, physical fatigue, social support available, and psychological well-being. Address the carers' needs by giving information, linking them with community services, discussing respite care, performing problem-solving counselling and teaching stress management.
